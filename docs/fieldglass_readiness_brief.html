How hard is Fieldglass, really?

The plumbing was easy and it is already done. What is hard is that some of these tenants are not ours to extract from at all, and the ones that are give us no stable client identity, no business clock and no history. None of that is fixable by engineering.

HFIG Data Vault accelerator · 5 September 2026 · every figure below is measured against 01_usnc_bronze_dev, not estimated

Getting Fieldglass bytes into the vault took about a week, and it reconciles to the row. That is the part “easy to integrate” describes, and it is finished.

The hub_job_request load went live on 4 September: 328,172 source rows in, 270,286 staged, 57,886 quarantined, holding exactly 7,230 distinct job requests. Nothing lost, nothing invented, every gate green.

Everything after that is blocked on decisions other people own. This page is what those decisions are, what they cost if we skip them, and what the whole programme looks like in quarters.

First, the number that reframes the question

Integrating Fieldglass perfectly does not give us client coverage. It gives us thirty clients.

Against a book of 200+ clients, every VMS we have put together covers roughly a quarter of them, and Fieldglass alone covers about fifteen percent. Nine further Fieldglass clients — everbank, lyondellbasell and seven others — have tables that hold zero rows.

So “Fieldglass is easy” and “Fieldglass gets us client coverage” are two different claims. Only the first is true.

And the second row of that table is not a rounding error. It is the largest single feed in the whole estate, and it sits in a separate schema because the client runs that tenant, not us.

Five blockers, and who owns each

Each has been raised. None can be closed by engineering effort on our side, and they are listed in the order they bite: whether we can get the data at all, then whether we can tell whose it is, then whether we can read it correctly.

  • We do not run every tenant, so not every tenant is ours to extract per client · commercial BRZ-13 asks this as a scope question — it is not one

    In some programmes the client runs the Fieldglass tenant and we participate in their instance rather than operating our own. The data is theirs to release, so getting it is a conversation with that client, not a pipeline change — and it is a separate conversation per client, with its own commercial and contractual answer.

    Bronze already reflects this. fieldglass_client_owned is a schema of its own, and the one client in it with data is Bayer, at 1,047,275 rows — larger than any tenant we manage. We have been treating “is the client-owned family in scope?” as a question about tables. It is a question about data rights, and the answer differs client by client.

    The classification does not exist anywhere we can read. Nothing in Bronze marks which of the thirty tenants we operate and which we do not, so we cannot size this from data. It is the one input on this page that has to come from the business.

    If we ship without it A worse failure than a missing client: a partial one. Where we are a participant we are likely to receive only the slice covering our own placements, not the whole programme — so a client-run tenant and a tenant we operate have different denominators. Added together and labelled “Fieldglass job postings”, the total is a number nobody can interpret, and it looks entirely normal.

  • The buyer column is not the client business decision BRZ-2 · answered, and the answer was no

    It holds whoever owns the VMS account, and that is not always the client. In the delphi_tech feed it is Guidant Global, Inc. — one of our own Impellam companies, running the programme for Delphi — across 12,459 rows. In commonspirit_health it is Dignity Health, the pre-merger name. The same feed also carries BorgWarner, who acquired Delphi Technologies.

    Three identities for one client, none of them stable. We asked Bronze for a stable code; there isn’t one. The BuyerId that exists is generated inside the reporting SQL Server, not by the VMS, and inside one tenant database there is no client id at all because everything in it belongs to one client.

    If we ship without it We key the vault on a display name. See the two silent failures below — both are already visible in today’s data.

  • A sixth of the feed arrives with no tenant Bronze BRZ-13

    57,380 of 328,172 rows — 17.5% — have a blank buyer. Rio Tinto alone contributes 15,335. Those rows are quarantined rather than loaded, which is the control working: we refuse to hash a null into the tenant position of a key. But nobody has explained why a sixth of a feed arrives without saying who it belongs to.

    If we ship without it We silently report on 82% of Fieldglass and call it Fieldglass.

  • There is no business clock Bronze BRZ-15

    Fieldglass ships no modification timestamp. All four candidates were measured over the 270,286 valid rows and every one is disqualified: job_posting_create_date and uploaded are constant per posting, job_posting_distribution_date is 42.4% null, and timestamp is a delivery clock frozen across 562 of the 1,127 postings that actually change.

    Without one, the satellite cannot tell a real change from a re-delivery. This is why the hub is live and the detail table is not. Bronze already emit a revision column on 62 of their worker tables; we have asked for the same here.

    If we ship without it Half the changed postings read as unchanged, and every version reads as current. Rate and requirement history would be wrong in a way no report would show.

  • There is no history — and it is being lost now Bronze · rebuild BRZ-14

    Every DOUBLE-typed column in the Fieldglass estate sits in a _backfill table — 153 instances, none in a live table — because those tables infer their types instead of declaring them. The consequence is concrete: 556 of 12,491 postal codes in one backfill are under five digits, because a DOUBLE cannot hold 00965.

    So the union deliberately excludes backfill, and Fieldglass history is not in the vault. This is the only blocker that is a rebuild rather than an answer, and the only one getting more expensive while it waits.

    If we ship without it Adding history later is a drop-and-reload of the Fieldglass satellite, not an append: change detection orders by load time, so late-arriving older versions would fabricate new ones.

Two ways this fails silently if we are told to ship anyway

Both are already present in the data. Neither raises an error, fails a gate, or looks wrong in a report.

A silent merge — two clients become one

The vault currently keys on buyer, so Delphi’s 12,459 job postings are stored under “Guidant Global, Inc.”. There is no collision today because Guidant appears in exactly one feed. The day a second Guidant-managed programme lands in Fieldglass, two different clients share one tenant key and merge into a single set of job requests. Every count, rate and margin computed across them is then wrong, and nothing distinguishes the merged rows from correct ones.

A silent re-key — one client becomes two

buyer is a display name, and display names change. When a client rebrands, merges, or is acquired, the key changes with it: the vault gains a second, unrelated client, and every historical row stays behind under the old one. We do not have to imagine this. commonspirit_health still carries “Dignity Health” and delphi_tech now carries “BorgWarner”. Both renames have already happened; we simply have not loaded through one yet.

The roll-up

Fieldglass for the tenants we run: about one quarter. All three vendor systems in the vault: two to three quarters. Gold trailing each vault domain by roughly a quarter — but startable today on the domains already loading, and not waiting on Fieldglass at all.

“Fieldglass complete” has no date, and cannot be given one from here. Every estimate above covers the tenants we operate. The rest arrive when each client agrees to release their data, one client at a time, and no amount of engineering capacity moves that. A schedule that promises all of Fieldglass by a date is promising something the data team does not control.

What these numbers assume. State them back to me if any is wrong — each one moves the estimate.

  • One engineer at the pace this project has actually run: 21 modelled entities, 38 source bindings and 10 source systems between 24 August and 5 September.
  • Every figure covers only the tenants we operate. We cannot yet say how many of the thirty those are — the distinction is commercial and appears nowhere in the data. If it turns out most Fieldglass clients run their own tenants, this stops being a data-engineering plan and becomes an account-management one.
  • Blockers answered within about two weeks of being asked. Recent evidence is mixed — a data question came back the same day, a permissions fix took four days, and two admin requests raised on 25 August are still open eleven days later.
  • BRZ-14 is a rebuild by the Bronze team, not a fix by us. If it is not prioritised, Fieldglass history does not arrive at all and the eventual reload gets larger every week.
  • Beeline is scoped at Fieldglass’s difficulty. It is probably worse: every one of its 12 client feeds carries both a code and a display name in the same column — KC alongside Kimberly-Clark alongside Kimberly-Clark Corp (Consolidated).

The thing worth deciding this week

Gold is not waiting on Fieldglass. The pressure to finish Fieldglass assumes it sits on the critical path to reporting. It does not. Gold does — and Gold is blocked on a single Unity Catalog grant.

03_usnc_gold_edm_dev exists, we hold no USE CATALOG on it, and four sibling gold catalogs are visible to us while ours is not. Until that is resolved, every consumer request has no answer regardless of how many vendor systems are loaded. The domains that could start today — GL journals, workers, Bullhorn job requests — are already live in the vault.

What we need from you

  1. The catalog grant. One permission, and it unblocks the layer business users actually read.

  2. Which tenants do we run? A list, thirty rows, one column: do we operate this Fieldglass instance or does the client. It is the cheapest thing on this page to produce and the only one that decides whether the rest of the plan is a quarter of work or a year of conversations. Where the answer is the client, we also need to know who owns that relationship and whether asking for the data is even on the table.

  3. A ruling on client identity. Is the feed name the client, or is buyer? They disagree in at least four feeds, and the mapping to finance is not one-to-one — bayer is at least two GP customers, BAYCL and BAYP. The mapping worksheet has 65 rows and none of them are filled in.

  4. Priority for BRZ-14 with the Bronze team. It is the one blocker that costs more the longer it waits.

  5. Agreement on what “done” means. If it means bytes in the vault, Fieldglass is close. If it means numbers someone can act on by client, it needs all four answers above first.
