One question · General ledger · Data & AI

Two journals are being discarded, and we do not think that is our call.

A data-quality rule in the warehouse drops any general-ledger line whose debit or credit is negative. Across 19 million GL rows that rule catches two journals. Whether it should is an accounting judgement rather than an engineering one, so we are putting the measurements in front of you without a recommendation. We have changed nothing: they are quarantined, not deleted, and every number below is measured rather than estimated.

The rule

It is governed configuration, not code, and it is currently set to drop — meaning a matching line never reaches the warehouse at all:

amount_not_negative  ·  action: drop  ·  COALESCE(debitamt >= 0 AND crdtamnt >= 0, TRUE)

It applies to the Great Plains general journal, open and closed year.

What it actually catches

Two journals, both from the BRPLM company database, both FY2025. They appear in both the open-year and closed-year tables:

JournalLinesNegative linesBalance Correcting / back-out / void flags
BRPLM 4960734 open / 2 closedall of them 0.00all zero
BRPLM 4961574 open / 2 closedall of them 0.00all zero

Every line in each journal is negative, so the rule does not unbalance them — it removes them entirely. Both balance to zero whether the negative lines are included or excluded, because excluding them leaves nothing.

They are not flagged as reversals. Great Plains carries correcting_je, back_out_je, original_je and voided, and all four are 0 on every line. Their transaction source is an ordinary GLTRX… entry.

One inconsistency worth knowing before you answer

The rule tests debitamt and crdtamnt. It does not test ordbtamt and orcrdamt — the originating-currency amounts — and those carry 16 negative values that pass through untouched and are sitting in the warehouse now.

So the estate currently accepts negative amounts in one pair of columns and discards whole journals for them in the other. Whichever way GL-1 is answered, those two treatments do not match today — which is why the question is not simply “are negatives valid?” but:

Why would a negative be acceptable in the originating amount and not in the functional one?

What we need decided

#QuestionWhat it changes
GL-1 Is a negative debit or credit a legitimate posting in this ledger — for example a correction posted in place rather than by swapping sides? If yes, the rule as written excludes valid postings and these two journals are absent from the warehouse. If no, they are correctly quarantined and we will say so in the reconciliation record.
GL-2 If they are legitimate, should the rule be removed, or downgraded from drop to warn so they load and stay visible? Drop keeps them out of the warehouse entirely; warn loads them and records that the rule matched. Either is straightforward to implement.
GL-3 Should ordbtamt / orcrdamt be treated the same way as the functional amounts, whichever way GL-1 goes? Today they are inconsistent. Either answer is fine; the mismatch is not.
GL-4 Are BRPLM 496073 and 496157 anything you recognise — a known correction, an import artefact, something already investigated? Two journals is few enough to look at individually, which is why we are asking rather than generalising.

Why we are asking now

The quarantined rows are also blocking a control. Our reconciliation gate refuses to attribute a quarantined row to a delivery it cannot identify, so while these two journals sit in quarantine, five downstream checks do not run. We could suppress that, and have deliberately not: the gate is right, and the honest fix is to settle whether the rows belong in the warehouse.

Nothing has been deleted. The rows are in a quarantine table with the rule that caught them recorded alongside. Whichever way this goes, they are recoverable.
