Q1Blocking

What date should the invoice carry?

Why us
Nothing. We compute the date in Databricks and freeze it at issue, so your copy and ours can never drift apart. GIE only renders the document.
What we need
The rule. Your template says "create the invoice date using the agreed date rule" without stating what it is.
Evidence
The sample suggests it behaved as the release date — Invoice Date had 1 distinct value across all 225 invoices while Accounting Date had 14. Plausible, but we would rather be told than infer it from one file. The code deliberately refuses to default it.
Q2Blocking

Can one client have two contracts in force at the same time?

Why it matters
It decides whether the client contract design works at all. We select the contract an invoice posts against from client + invoice date. If a client can have two at once, that does not identify a contract and we need a third term — service type, or the contract named on the order.
Your own words
“this may change as we go through the legal entity unification only where we have a mix of services and one is professional services (SOW)” — which reads like yes.
If two are ever open
We will refuse to pick rather than choose by row order. An invoice posted against the wrong contract is not something a later report would catch.
Q3Answered — thank you

MSP on expenses now rejects, across all clients

Your answer
“MSP fee's should not be applied on expenses across all clients and therefore we should expect a record to reject where this is present in the data.”
Built
Done. An MSP fee on an expense line is rejected with a reason a person can act on. An absent or zero fee is not a rejection — 39 of your 419 invoices are wholly expenses, so treating a missing fee as a present one would reject the normal case.
Worth saying
We had measured this and deliberately not built it, because you had said the variety was real. Measuring could not settle it and asking did — but the measurement is what made the question specific enough to answer.
Q3bWith you

Misc fees — per entry, or per client?

Your open question
“For Misc Fee's it's slightly different they may or may not attract an MSP fee (this is a newer feature). I have asked whether this is something applied per misc invoice entry or if it's at a client level.”
Why it matters to us
The two answers need different things built, so it is worth knowing before rather than after. Some with, some without means we pass the data through and build no rule. Q7Time-sensitive

Can the Workday contract record carry the Hubspot deal id?

Background
Already answered: Hubspot is the main system for deal and commercial data, so it decides contract value, dates and stage. Workday is authoritative for the contract identifier — the thing the posting must carry. Clean split, and we have built to it.
What it creates
The two halves now have to be joinable, and today nothing joins them. Hubspot deals key on their own id; Workday contracts are a reference id against a customer. There is no shared key.
Three ways out
(a) the contract id already exists as a Hubspot property and is simply not replicated to us — cheapest if true, and only the CRM team can say; (b) Databricks replicates the id between Hubspot and Workday, which removes the problem rather than answering it, because we would then hold both ids by construction; (c) Workday's record carries the Hubspot deal id when it is keyed in.
What we checked
It is not in the Hubspot data we hold. Deals carry 35 columns and the only identifier is the deal's own id — no order, contract or quote field, and nothing hidden in the unmapped-field capture either. But we only see what is replicated to us, and 35 properties is plainly a subset, so it may well exist in Hubspot itself.
Why now
(c) is nearly free while the process is manual and being designed this minute, and expensive afterwards. The alternative nobody should accept is matching contracts to deals on client and dates — an inference, on money, of exactly the kind that goes wrong quietly.
Note on (b)
It is the best outcome and not the cheapest, and it runs against the direction now agreed: both Hubspot and Workday write into Databricks, and Workday's reference values will land with us as Workday reference data. Everything we do with Workday today is read. Sending an id back the other way is a new posture needing a credential arrangement we have asked Platform for, and an owner. Which makes (a) materially more attractive if the property already exists in Hubspot.

For Eva and the CRM team

Hubspot turned out to answer two things we had written off.

Context, briefly

We had concluded there was no source anywhere for client company structure, and we were wrong — Hubspot has it. companies.hs_parent_company_id gives client parent/child: Aerotek and TEKsystems both roll up to Allegis Group, Aetna to CVS Health. That is exactly what is needed to answer “what does this client group spend with us in total”. deals also carries contract value and contract dates, which is one end of the new deal → quote → contract process.

We have not changed anything in Hubspot and are not asking for anything to be changed. Both questions are about meaning, not access.

Q11Blocking

How does a Fieldglass buyer code map to a Hubspot company?

Why it blocks
This is the only thing standing between us and client-group spend reporting. The hierarchy is in Hubspot; the spend is in Fieldglass; nothing joins them.
The problem
Spend attaches at a 4-character buyer code. Of the 12 buyer codes in the invoice feed, only one carries a company name at all — the other eleven are blank.
What we will not do
TEKS looks like TEKsystems. We are recording that as a guess and nothing more: matching companies by name resemblance is exactly how you end up merging two real clients into one, and every total above it would still look plausible.
Ideal answer
A list — buyer code to Hubspot company id — even a partial one. Twelve rows would unblock it today.
Q12Meaning

Is one Hubspot deal one client contract?

What we see
A deal carries total_contract_value, contract_start_date and end_date_contract, which reads like one deal is one contract.
What gives us pause
15,430 deals against 2,457 companies — about six per company. We cannot tell how many became contracts, or whether a renewal is a new deal or an edit to the old one.
Why it matters
It decides the grain we bind the contract data at. Getting it wrong would double-count contract value.
