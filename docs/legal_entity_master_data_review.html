The headline: the governance model moves, the questions do not disappear

Amy’s central point is that legal entities should be governed as master data in the Global Data Catalogue, through the Trading Entity and Master Data governance model, rather than being driven by the vault design. We accept that without reservation, and it is the right correction: it moves where each decision is taken. It does not remove the need to take it, and one consequence lands on us directly — the vault’s legal-entity business key becomes an authored identifier owned by a register that does not exist yet.

That validates leaving our legal-entity bindings as PLACEHOLDER rather than inventing a key. It is the outcome our own plan already forbade us from working around, so nothing is wasted by having waited.

Settled — no further discussion needed

Recorded against each ask in its own document. Listed here so Monday is spent only on what is open.
WDJ-10

Cost centres are global — and this one was load-bearing

Closed
The answer
A cost centre is globally governed reference data with one consistent business meaning. Regional, legal-entity and management reporting go through separate dimensions — Legal Entity, Region, Company, Business Unit, Worktags — never by reusing a code with a second meaning. High confidence, designed alongside Alex in group finance.
Why this one mattered more than the others
It closes at exactly the condition we had written down: a global dimension may be keyed on its code alone only once someone has confirmed the code means one thing group-wide. Had the answer been the other way and we keyed on the code regardless, two regional cost centres would have collapsed into one hub row, every gate would have stayed green, and the only symptom would have been totals quietly wrong one level up. A silent defect is now retired rather than merely unobserved.
BRZ-2

No client reassignment in Bronze for the GP journal

Closed, scoped
The answer
VMS data enters 1Staff/GP where client and finance customer relationships already exist; the journals pass through to Workday, so Bronze never assigns or reassigns a client.
Keep the scope attached to it
The reasoning leans on unification moving the group toward two trading brands — while Amy’s own headline is that brand unification and legal entity unification are different things, with entities active and reportable until formally dissolved. Consistent, but only because this closure is about the GP journal. Detached from that scope it would read as a claim about how many legal entities exist.
LEG-7

CBO Office owns the register; CPTO implements

Answered
Why this is the cleanest answer in the set
It names an owner, separates the decision from the implementation, and gives us a route for change requests that does not depend on knowing who to ask. Decisions may originate in SLT, Board, Finance or Legal; CBO governs the process and holds the evidence; CPTO implements and does not own the structure. All downstream systems, Workday included, consume the approved structure.
LEG-8

Effective From is the real-world legal date, and blank beats a default

Answered
The answer, and why we like the second half
Incorporation, legal rename, ownership transfer, merger, dissolution — not the date a row was entered or loaded. Where unknown it stays blank or is flagged, never defaulted to a load date. That is the two-clock rule stated by the business in its own words, and it matches the vault: applied_dts is business-effective, load_dts is ours. A defaulted date is indistinguishable from a known one once stored, which is why the instruction not to default is the valuable half.
LEG-5

Ownership and contract are two different edges

Answered
The answer
Impellam PLC may own Guidant Global, but Guidant Global is the trading entity and solely holds the contracts with clients, workers and suppliers. A global client may contract with different trading entities across jurisdictions, and each individual contract links to exactly one. Two relationships in the model, not one — which is the structure we would have wanted anyway.

Four to settle on Monday

Three are places where the written answer and the sample record disagree. That is worth saying plainly: the prose is right in each case.
LEG-1

Is the UK in HF-UK-001 permanent, or current?

Decide first
What was answered, and it is a good answer
The Global Data Catalogue owns the permanent identifier. The sample shows HF-UK-001 — an HFIG-assigned code, with Workday’s UK011 kept beside it as a system reference rather than as the identity. That is better than the option we floated of adopting Workday’s code outright, and it dissolves the objection we raised against it: Workday does not become the system of record for company identity, and we still get the crosswalk.
The question
A permanent identifier must not be derived from an attribute that can change. If UK is the entity’s current jurisdiction, then re-domiciliation forces a choice between changing a permanent ID and leaving it asserting something false — and the same answer requires the ID to survive the transition and “retain historical reporting integrity”. If UK is a minting namespace, fixed when the entity is first registered and never revisited, it is sound.
Why it cannot be left implicit
The two readings look identical until the first entity moves jurisdiction, and by then the identifier is in every downstream system. We have just been through this exact argument on WDJ-2, where a Databricks hash was proposed as a permanent external journal ID: it was rejected because its value depends on inputs that can change. Same principle, different column.
What we need
One sentence in the BRD saying which it is.
LEG-3

Ownership: the prose says separate and dated, the sample says two columns

Prose is right
The disagreement
The written answer holds ownership separately from the master record, effective dated, with parent, subsidiary, ownership percentage, dates and status. The sample puts Parent Legal Entity and Ultimate Parent on the entity record as plain attributes.
Why the difference matters
Attributes on the record cannot carry ownership history — and an ownership-transfer date appears in Amy’s own LEG-8 list of real-world effective dates, so history is explicitly in scope. Separately, Ultimate Parent is derivable from the parent chain; storing it as its own field invites the two to drift, and the stored value is the one people will report from.
What we need
Confirmation that the BRD carries the prose version. We read the mockup as a one-card simplification rather than a change of position.
LEG-6

Classification and Business Role overlap — which governs?

Define
The overlap
Classification is single-select (Trading / Holding / Funding) and drives “reporting, consolidation and novation treatment”. Business Role is multi-valued (Trading, Employing, Billing, Payroll). The sample shows Classification TRADING and role Trading Entity: Yes.
The questions
What does Classification Holding with Trading Entity: Yes mean, and which field governs when they disagree? Our original question was whether a company plays more than one role; the flags answer it as yes, which makes the flags the operative answer and leaves Classification needing a tighter definition than the roles it appears to duplicate.
Why we care about the answer, not the aesthetics
If Classification drives consolidation treatment, it is a reporting control, not a label — and a control that can contradict a neighbouring field is one somebody will eventually resolve by guessing.
LEG-2

Legacy Finance Code is singular, and we have evidence it cannot be

Add a field
The evidence, from our own data and from Workday’s
Two legacy ledgers feed the vault today — Great Plains and UKG, with BRPLM our only UKG company. And Workday’s own cost-centre data carries five retired source families, which is what prior migrations leave behind:
zSAGE 207 zUKG 158 zEH 100 zAFAS 79 zCOST 58 (602 of 666 cost centres)
The consequence
A single Legacy Finance Code cannot hold codes from several legacy ledgers at once. Either it becomes a small repeating structure of (source system, code), or the field silently means “whichever legacy code someone entered first” — and a crosswalk that is right for GP and wrong for UKG is worse than one that is absent, because it will be used.
Why this is worth the field
These two columns are the answer to a mapping we could not complete. We reconstructed the GP–Workday company crosswalk by name similarity: 11 of our organisations against 82 Workday companies, exact code overlap zero, five *SPV pairings we flagged openly as guesses, and BARM and CSS unknown. Populated properly, this block retires that guesswork entirely.
