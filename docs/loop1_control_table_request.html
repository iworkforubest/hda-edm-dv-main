  • We create the control schema and its four tables — not you. It does not exist in 01_usnc_bronze_dev today, and the first version of this page asked you to stand it up. Measured 31 August: our deploying identity already holds CREATE_SCHEMA on that catalog through scope_tds_full_scopes_write, so asking you for it was asking for something we can do ourselves. The DDL is generated from our control standard and committed at governance/control_objects_bronze.sql: the three mandatory audit tables every layer carries — aud_load_run, aud_table_load, aud_table_discard, append-only, they record what happened and must never be rewritten — plus ctl_delivery_manifest, which is mutable because a manifest records what should happen and stays correctable. Creating them is not writing to them. The rows below stay yours: only the delivering system knows what it delivered, and control_contracts/bronze.yaml remains the contract for exactly that.
  • Write one row to 01_usnc_bronze_dev.control.ctl_delivery_manifest — manifest_id (any stable id for the batch), delivered_count (how many rows it delivered), delivered_at, delivered_by and source_system.

What the delivery manifest does, and what it does not do

Writing ctl_delivery_manifest gives us the cross-layer delivery control — did Silver stage everything you delivered. That is worth having, and it is the honest thing for Bronze to state, because it is a fact about what Bronze did.

It does not, on its own, light up loop-1, and an earlier version of this page implied it would. Loop-1 compares what landed in one Silver table against a count taken before the load, and for three of the four feeds below our loader collapses re-delivered rows: gl20000 carries about 1.8 copies of every business row across 7 deliveries, ukg_raw.gl exactly 2 across 2. So your delivered_count sits above what our loader accepts — by design, not by error — and the two numbers are not interchangeable.

What loop-1 needs from you is item 3, the _manifest_id stamp. The count it compares against has to be taken after our deduplication, which is ours to compute, not yours.

Updated 27 August. The identity gained a superseded term when we built a way to correct a rejected row and re-land it. Nothing changes in what we are asking you for — the term counts rejects we later accepted, and it is ours to record, not yours.

This is the only check we run that proves everything that was supposed to arrive did. Every other gate checks the shape or the content of what arrived. Without this one, a load that silently dropped rows reports success and is indistinguishable from a complete one.

What still has to happen on our side

So that "done" is honest, and so nobody waits on the wrong team: once you deliver the ids, the full identity still cannot be evaluated until we apply one piece of DDL of our own. The superseded term needs control.ctl_quarantine_superseded, which is written and merged but not yet created in the lake — five of our six control tables exist, that one does not. Applying it is a single task run on our job and does not depend on you.

In other words: your part unblocks the reconciliation, and one task run of ours completes it. Neither of us is waiting on the other.

What we are not asking for

Nothing about our model. delivered_count is a count of the rows in your batch, nothing more. We only reconcile targets that take one row per approved row. Our hubs deduplicate — 4,444,172 GP rows become 2,221,108 hub rows — and our satellites store only changed rows, so the identity is false for them by design and the gate refuses to reconcile them at all.

Nothing about ref_dq_expectation. It is created and empty, and that is fine: the generator falls back to its two compiled-in key-safety rules, so nothing is blocked. It exists for when a data steward has a rule to add. It has since moved to the control schema with the manifest, and gained a severity column — still nothing for you.
