First, a correction to what we told you

Our earlier figure was wrong, and we should say so plainly

We previously reported that keeping every row would overstate three invoices by around 44,050. That number was real arithmetic on the wrong data. The Bronze table we measured turned out to hold three separate loads, and two of them are synthetic test data that someone loaded during platform testing — 35 rows carrying fields no real invoice has, including one run literally named feeder_test_1.

Those 35 test rows are the three invoices and essentially all of the 44,050. There was never a real overstatement. We have now excluded them, and the live extract stands at 419 invoices and 1,152,842.86.

A worker claiming three meals at 45.00 on one expense sheet is entirely ordinary. The reason they look identical to us is that Fieldglass's line reference (AEE1ES00007656) identifies the expense sheet, not the individual claim on it — so three receipts on one sheet all arrive under the same reference with nothing to tell them apart. There is no hidden field we are missing; we checked all 169 columns.

So we would like to change the recommendation

Please don't remove them — and we no longer think we should build the removal step

You agreed to a process where duplicates are surfaced for manual review and then selected for removal. On this evidence, that process would delete legitimate expense claims: someone reviewing three identical food lines at 45.00 has no way to tell a genuine third meal from an accidental repeat, and the invoice total says all three are genuine.

We would rather build the report without the delete button: a weekly list of invoices carrying repeated expense lines, so you can see them and raise anything that looks wrong with the supplier at source. If a real duplicate ever does occur, it is a Fieldglass data issue to correct there, not something to quietly drop on the way through. Does that work for you?

The Gross and Net column names are the wrong way round

Net_Invoice_Amount is the larger figure — it is gross including tax. Gross_Invoice_Amount is the net. You spotted this on your example invoice; we can now say it is true of the whole feed. It stays hidden because only 2 of the 419 invoices carry any tax at all, so on the other 417 the two columns are identical. Worth flagging to anyone who reads these columns by name.

Which total should the invoice to Ameren carry?

380 of the 419 invoices carry MSP lines, totalling 24,054.04. The client-facing total is supplier lines plus MSP margin; the supplier total alone is 1,152,842.86.

We need to know which figure the generated invoice should show and reconcile against — the client total including MSP margin, or the supplier total. The two differ by the margin on 380 invoices, so getting it wrong is not a rounding matter. Everything else is built; this is the one answer we need before we can generate the first invoice.

And how is Guidant Global Inc spelled on the register?

Not a pedantic question, unfortunately. Our records currently carry Guidant Global, Inc. — with a comma and a trailing period — because that is the exact string the Delphi Technologies Fieldglass feed delivers in its buyer field. You have written Guidant Global Inc.

A feed tells us what that feed sends, not how a company is registered, so we would rather ask than pick: on the invoice document to Ameren AP and on the Workday posting, the registered name is the name. Whichever of the two is correct, we will use it everywhere and stop carrying the other.
