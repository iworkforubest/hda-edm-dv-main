Which companies are we, and who owns whom?

Eight questions. None of them is a data question — every one is a fact about the group that only Business and Legal hold, and the data model is finished and waiting on them. The modelling is done and merged; it currently loads nothing, because a company register is not something engineering can derive.

Since this was written: the register may already exist, and so may the hierarchy

Retrieved from the Workday implementation tenant on 6 September: 82 companies, each with a code and a registered-style name, across 16 jurisdictions. The worksheet we sent you has 24 rows and asks you to fill four identifying columns; not one of the 96 cells is filled, and Workday appears to hold most of the answer already.

This does not answer the questions below, and we are not withdrawing them. Three reasons, and each is a question for you rather than a caveat we can settle:

  • A Workday Company is a financial reporting entity. It is usually one legal entity, and it is not guaranteed to be — LEG-2 is exactly that question.
  • The values came from an implementation tenant. Configured reference IDs usually migrate to production, and usually is not something we will build a key on.
  • It supplies a code and a name. It does not supply a company registration number — which is LEG-1’s second option.

And a correction to our own first reading, made 7 September. We told you Workday held nothing about ownership. It appears to hold a good deal: alongside the 82 companies sit 30 consolidation groups, each named for a holding company.

Corporate Services Group Holding Inc ... Star Apple Holding BV Consolidated Staffing MS Holding BV Consolidated ... and 20 more

A consolidation group is how Workday rolls companies up for reporting, so the membership of those 30 groups is an ownership hierarchy — the thing LEG-3 through LEG-6 exist to establish.

We have tried to read it, and we are one permission away. The call that returns the membership is Get_Organizations, and on 7 September it answered:

SOAP-ENV:Server.processingError | The task submitted is not authorized.

That is a permission refusal rather than a broken request — a deliberately malformed request to the same service comes back as a validation error instead, so Workday accepted ours and then declined it. The integration user we hold is cleared for the reference lists and not for organisations. Asked of the Workday team as WDJ-5; if it is granted, LEG-3 to LEG-6 may need nothing from you at all.

Which makes one of those names a question in itself. All Headfirst Global Plc Consolidated reads like an apex, and Impellam Group Limited Consolidated sits beside it as a peer. Whether one already contains the other is exactly what we cannot see — and it is the same structure the coming brand unification has to produce. If Workday already models it, that is a considerably better starting point than a spreadsheet.

What it changes is LEG-1, LEG-7 and now possibly LEG-3–LEG-6, and all of them for the better. See the notes on each below. Before anyone fills in the worksheet, it is worth confirming what Workday already holds — we would rather you did not hand-write 96 cells we can retrieve.

There is a deadline, and it is not ours

Impellam Group and HeadFirst Group unify under a new brand in the coming weeks. The model has been built so that a rebrand costs one new company row, two ownership rows and two dates — no key changes, no reload, and every historical figure still rolls up the way it did when it was recorded.

That only holds if the register exists before the rebrand. Built afterwards, the same change means reconstructing which company owned what on which date, from feeds that only ever carried a display name. LEG-1 and LEG-7 are the two that cannot slip.

LEG-1

What identifies a legal entity, permanently?

Blocks everything
Ask
Choose one of two, and tell us which:
  • (a) HFIG assigns a code per company — ours to define, ours to keep unique.
  • (b) The identifier is the pair (jurisdiction, company number) — externally verifiable, and already unique by construction.
Why it matters
This value is hashed into every key in the vault that touches a legal entity. Changing it later is not an edit; it is a re-key of the whole family, and every historical row is stranded behind the old value.
The trap
It must not be the company’s name. That is exactly the defect this work exists to fix — see the note below.
A third option, found 6 September
(c) Workday’s Company_Reference_ID — UK011, NL010, AU100. It already exists for 82 companies, it is one column, it carries a jurisdiction prefix, and it survives a rebrand exactly as (a) would. Its weakness is that it makes Workday the system of record for company identity, which is a governance decision rather than a technical one.
Our lean
Now (c), where a Workday company really is one legal entity. It is (a) without the work of assigning codes ourselves, and without a second register to keep in step. Failing that, (b): a registration number never changes when a company rebrands, and a rebrand is what is coming. It makes the key two columns instead of one, so it is a decision rather than a detail.
Done when
One sentence naming (a) or (b).
Answered 8 Sep — option (a), and it neutralises our objection to (c)
Amy: legal entities are master data in the Global Data Catalogue, which owns the permanent identifier and governs creation, change and retirement. The sample record shows HF-UK-001 — so this is option (a), an HFIG-assigned code, with Workday’s UK011 kept alongside as a system reference rather than as the identity. That is a better answer than (c) and it dissolves the objection we raised against it: Workday stops being the system of record for company identity while we keep the crosswalk.

One question back, and it is WDJ-2 in a different costume. HF-UK-001 carries a jurisdiction inside a permanent identifier. Amy’s own answer requires the ID to survive the whole transition and “retain historical reporting integrity”. If UK is the current jurisdiction, re-domiciliation forces a choice between changing a permanent ID and leaving it asserting something false. If it is a minting namespace fixed at creation and never revisited, it is sound — but that has to be stated, because the two readings look identical until the first entity moves. A permanent external identifier must not be derived from an attribute that can change — the same argument that corrected WDJ-2.
LEG-7

Who owns the register, and where does it live?

Blocks everything
Ask
A named owner, and an agreed home the vault can read on a schedule — a maintained table, a file on a known path, or an export from whatever system already holds this.
Why it matters
A spreadsheet answered once decays from the day it is filled in. Companies are incorporated, renamed, redomiciled and struck off; each is a change the vault must see. An unowned register is worse than none, because people trust it.
Also needed
Does something already hold this — a statutory register, a group structure chart, a Companies House export? We would rather read an existing source than create a second one.
And it may be answered
Workday. We can retrieve the company list on a schedule through its Reference Service, which makes the “maintained, with an owner, in a place a job can read” problem somebody else’s solved problem rather than a new spreadsheet. If Workday is the system of record for company identity, say so and this question closes. What Workday does not hold is the ownership hierarchy, so LEG-3 onwards still need an owner.
Done when
A name, and a location we can point a job at.
Answered 8 Sep — CBO Office owns it, CPTO implements it
Amy: ownership of the register sits with the CBO Office. Create, modify, merge, retire and dissolve decisions may originate in SLT, Board, Finance or Legal, but CBO governs the end-to-end process and holds the approvals, controls and evidence. CPTO implements approved changes and does not own the structure, and all downstream systems including Workday consume the approved structure from the governed source. Amy is building a framework for how requests and changes move business → CBO → CPTO as part of the BRD. This is the cleanest answer in the set: it names an owner, separates the decision from the implementation, and gives us a route for change requests that does not depend on knowing who to ask.

Why the name cannot be the identifier — the evidence, in one column

Every VMS feed carries an account-holder name in a column called buyer. Measured across the estate, that one column holds three different kinds of thing:

Albertsons Companies, Inc the client, current name Dignity Health a SUPERSEDED client name, still delivered years on Guidant Global, Inc. one of OURS -- Guidant runs the programme, so holds the account BorgWarner SAME feed as Guidant -- the company that acquired Delphi Technologies

One feed, three identities, none stable. That is why the register is keyed on something that does not move, and why the registered name is recorded as an attribute — so that when it changes, we get a new version rather than a new company.

LEG-2

Four facts per company, for 24 candidates

The bulk
Ask
For every row of legal_entity_roster_worksheet.csv:
Columns
  • registered_name — as registered, not as traded. “Lorien” is a brand; the company behind it has a longer name.
  • company_number — the registration number in its jurisdiction.
  • jurisdiction — already filled in for all 24; confirm rather than re-derive.
  • registered_status — active, dormant, dissolved. Recorded, never filtered: a dissolved company still owns history.
Why four
These are exactly the attributes the vault can receive. Anything else has nowhere to go today, so please tell us before adding a column rather than after.
Done when
24 rows with all four filled, or a row marked as not a company with a reason.
Answered 8 Sep — four attributes accepted, more required, and the sample is an image
Amy: the four proposed (Registered Name, Registration Number, Jurisdiction, Status) “can form part of the core dataset”, but legal entity governance, ownership, finance alignment, novation planning and lifecycle management need more. Authoritative sources should be exhausted before anyone is asked to fill a cell by hand, and the entity list should be validated against the future-state strategy before population begins. The catalogue is business-owned and explicitly not constrained by the vault design, which we accept — it moves where the decision is taken, not whether it has to be taken.

LEG-3

Who owns whom — 23 ownership edges

The bulk
Ask
One row per ownership relationship, not one per company — the shape differs from the worksheet:
Columns
parent, child, effective_from, relationship_status
Why dated
So that “who owned this in March” and “who owns it today” are both answerable from one table. An edge that ends is a new row saying so, never an edit — which is what makes the history trustworthy.
Note
23, not 24: the group parent has no parent. After the rebrand it will have one, and that is two more rows rather than a restructure.
Done when
23 edges with dates.
Answered 8 Sep — agreed in principle, and the sample contradicts the prose
Amy agrees ownership is held separately from the master record and effective dated, supporting parent, subsidiary, ownership percentage, effective dates and status — and not limited to the 24-entity view.

The mockup does the opposite. It puts Parent Legal Entity and Ultimate Parent on the entity record as plain attributes, which cannot carry ownership history — and an ownership-transfer date is in Amy’s own LEG-8 list of real-world effective dates. Ultimate Parent is also derivable from the parent chain, so storing it invites drift between the two. The prose is right and the mockup is a simplification; the question for Monday is only which one the BRD will carry.
LEG-8

What date is effective_from?

Cheap now
Ask
Confirm it is the date the change took effect in the world — the incorporation, the rename, the transfer — and not the date somebody typed it in.
Why we ask explicitly
Four days ago the same question went unasked on another feed and cost a week. All four candidate timestamps turned out to be unusable, and the answer became a request to another team. A maintained register has no such excuse: whoever records the change knows when it took effect.
If unknown
Say so per row rather than supplying the load date. A missing date we can handle; a wrong one silently mis-dates every figure that rolls up through it.
Done when
One sentence, plus per-row exceptions if any.
Answered 8 Sep — real-world legal date, and blank beats a default
Amy: Effective From is the date the change became legally effective in the real world — incorporation, legal rename, ownership transfer, merger, dissolution — and explicitly not the date a record was entered or loaded. Where it is unknown the field stays blank or is flagged for investigation rather than defaulting to a load date, because “historical integrity is more important than completeness where evidence is unavailable”.

That is the two-clock rule stated by the business in its own words, and it matches what the vault already does: applied_dts is the business-effective date and load_dts is ours. The instruction not to default a missing value is the part worth holding on to — a defaulted date is indistinguishable from a known one once it is stored.

LEG-4

Are these the right 24?

Scope
Ask
Two checks on the candidate list.
Check one
Three brands are confirmed Impellam but appear nowhere on the group’s own operating-company page: barpellam (GB), irishrecruitment (IE), staffingms (US). Are they companies, dormant names, or trading names of something already listed? Each is a different answer for the ownership tree.
Check two
A candidate is one brand in one country. That may still be several companies — a holding company and a trading company, or one per legal purpose. If so the register is larger than 24 and we would rather know now.
Done when
A yes, or a corrected list.
Answered 8 Sep — the 24 are not assumed complete, and the roster is Amy’s action
Amy: the list must be validated against the Legal Entity Unification programme, future-state trading entity design and statutory registrations. Trading brand, operating brand, legal entity and finance reporting entity are not necessarily 1:1 — Barpellam, Irish Recruitment and Staffing MS are named as needing that test specifically. The catalogue should hold the complete population including dormant, holding, employing and reporting entities, with future-state entities identified separately from current-state. This answers our question by refusing its premise, which is the right answer — we asked “are these the right 24?” and the answer is that the number is an output of work not yet done.
LEG-5

Does a company play more than one role?

Shape
Ask
The model records ownership. Is there a second relationship we also need — most likely “operates the programme for”, which is how Guidant Global relates to Delphi Technologies?
Why it changes the build
If yes, it is a separate structure rather than a column, so that a question about ownership cannot accidentally return operating relationships too. That is a small addition, made once, and awkward to retrofit.
Done when
Either “ownership only”, or a named second relationship and what it means.
Answered 8 Sep — two relationships, not one
Amy: legal ownership and contractual relationship are different edges. Impellam PLC may own Guidant Global, but Guidant Global is the trading entity and solely holds the contracts with clients, workers and suppliers. A global client may contract with different trading entities across jurisdictions, and each individual contract links to exactly one trading legal entity.

That is two links in the model, which is what we would have wanted structurally — ownership parent-to-subsidiary, and a separate contractual edge from trading entity to client, worker and supplier. Nothing here forces a change to the vault; it forces the catalogue to supply both edges rather than one.
LEG-6

How does a company tie back to finance?

Shape
Ask
The rule mapping a legal entity to its customer records in Dynamics GP (RM00101, keyed on custnmbr within a company database).
Why it is not a lookup
It is measurably not one-to-one. The bayer feed already corresponds to at least two GP customers, BAYCL and BAYP. So the mapping is either one-to-many, or it needs a rule deciding which customer a given row belongs to — and that rule is a business decision, not a join.
Done when
A stated rule. “One legal entity, many customers” is a complete answer if that is the truth.
Answered 8 Sep — GP’s customer structure is not the model, and is under review
Amy: the current GP customer structure must not be assumed to represent the legal or contractual structure. Some clients are a single GP customer record for operational simplicity only. The model needs one trading legal entity to many customer records, with customer records treated as supporting reference data that may be restructured without changing the underlying legal entity relationship. Amy and the North America finance team will review the GP customer structure and design the future-state hierarchy for Workday migration — so this is now an owned action rather than an open question.
